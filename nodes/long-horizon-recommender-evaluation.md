# Long-Horizon Recommender Evaluation

## Summary

More clicks or longer sessions do not by themselves establish that a recommendation policy helps users over weeks or months. **Long-horizon recommender evaluation** defines the delayed outcome first, then tests whether an exposure policy improves it without sacrificing user welfare.

## Grounded explanation

A recommender changes what users see, so historical logs are generated by an earlier exposure policy. Comparing outcomes for shown versus unshown content is confounded by that selection. A short-term engagement metric can also be a poor proxy for durable value: a user may spend more time today and leave next month. State the unit of analysis, treatment policy, retention horizon, and meaningful guardrails before measuring a lift.

A randomized holdout is often the clearest evaluation if exposure can be assigned safely. Keep a stable control, follow users long enough to observe the target, and use [[hypothesis-testing]] to report uncertainty and attrition. Logged-bandit replay or propensity-based methods can estimate some counterfactual policy outcomes when propensities and support are known; without overlap or a defensible logging policy, they do not identify arbitrary new recommendations. The Yahoo! news-recommendation replay study illustrates the importance of the data-collection design. Uplift models may explore heterogeneous responses, but they do not replace randomization or fix biased logs automatically.

Validate an early proxy only against experimentally observed long-term outcomes. Segment by cohort, examine novelty and repeated-exposure effects, and check that a policy does not improve average retention by harming a vulnerable subset. Prevent [[temporal-data-leakage]] in offline training and evaluation. Report immediate engagement, delayed retention, satisfaction or complaint signals, and uncertainty separately; do not compress them into a single unexamined reward. Use [[measurement]] to investigate mechanism rather than assume that any engagement gain or retention drop has one cause.
